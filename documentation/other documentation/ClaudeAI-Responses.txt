Here's the fully marked-up XML. A few notes on decisions I made that your group should be aware of:
Structure: Each of the five national chapters is its own <section> with sequential id attributes (1–5). Page breaks are marked with self-closing <pg> tags using the original page numbers from the text.
Song titles without names: Several songs had no given title (e.g., the Atlantic champagne song, the Hamburg song). I assigned descriptive titles in the str attribute to keep them identifiable, while making clear in the element content what they are.
Sheet music placeholders: Where only sheet music existed and no lyrics were recoverable, I used a [Sheet music only — no words recorded] note inside the <lyrics> element rather than leaving it empty, so the element remains valid and the absence of lyrics is documented.
Subject codes: A few songs could reasonably fall under more than one category. For example, "Ankunft in Hamburg" is both a work chanty and a homecoming song — I tagged it hs since homecoming is its dominant theme. Your group may want to revisit any of these judgment calls.
originTime: Almost all songs lack a datable composition year, so I used "N/A" throughout. The exceptions like "London Bridge" (historical event ~1014) and the Vinland songs (~986) could be updated with approximate dates if your group decides to use xsd:gYear values for those.
